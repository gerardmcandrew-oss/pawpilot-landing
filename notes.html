A

Assigned case

Case H: Waggy Tails Dog Daycare, Lucan, Dublin. Waggy Tails is the fictional business supplied by the assignment. It is run by its owner, Aisling Nolan.

Capacity40 dog places per day
TeamSix staff
BookingsArrive by WhatsApp, Instagram and text
DemandMondays and Fridays overbooked about twice a month; Wednesdays at roughly half capacity
VaccinationsPaper records; a dog with an expired kennel cough vaccination was recently admitted
Owner messagesAbout 50 a day asking how dogs are doing

These figures describe the fictional case business only. They are not customer research, Irish industry statistics or PawPilot results.

Problem statement. Waggy Tails relies on disconnected processes for managing bookings, vaccination records and owner communication. This creates booking confusion, makes vaccination eligibility harder to track and interrupts staff throughout the day.

Design challenge. How might we give smaller dog daycare operators one simple digital system for managing bookings, checking vaccination eligibility and keeping owners updated without creating additional administration?

B

Hunch (before AI)

Hunch (before AI)

I think smaller dog daycare businesses could benefit from a simple digital system that keeps track of bookings, vaccination dates and daily dog updates, rather than having staff manage everything through messages and paperwork.

This is the hunch I started from, before using any AI tools. Everything after it was built to test it.

C

Ideas explored

Starting from my hunch, I used AI-assisted brainstorming to consider these product directions:

  • Booking and capacity management.
  • Vaccination reminders and eligibility.
  • Daily dog activity updates.
  • Reducing repeated owner enquiries.
  • Improving visibility of quieter booking days.
  • Combining the connected operational workflows.

These were directions explored during brainstorming, not six separate hunches written before AI was used.

Why the combined product was selected. The problems are connected. A booking-only system would not solve vaccination eligibility or owner enquiries. A vaccination-only system would leave booking administration unchanged. An owner-update-only system would not prevent overbooking. Each one depends on knowing which dogs are booked for which day, so it made sense to build them around the same records.

D

Selected solution and reasoning

PawPilot combines bookings, vaccination checks and owner updates. These are connected parts of the same daily operation: a booking decides who attends, a vaccination record decides whether they can attend, and the day's activities become the owner's update.

A narrowly focused product for smaller daycare operators was chosen rather than a general-purpose business management system. Smaller teams need the few jobs they repeat every day to be quick and dependable, more than they need a long list of modules.

This is a working product hypothesis. The prototype shows the idea can work in a browser. It does not show that daycare owners would pay for it.

F

Technology type

PawPilot is proposed dog daycare operations software. The current alpha, demonstrated as Waggy Tails, is a single web page built with:

  • HTML.
  • CSS.
  • JavaScript.
  • Browser localStorage, which keeps bookings, check-ins and activities in the user's own browser.

It runs on GitHub Pages with no server or database. It is not yet a secure cloud service: there are no accounts, data is not shared between devices, and nothing is backed up.

Five-line lean PRD

  1. Hypothesis: Independent daycare operators may value one straightforward system for bookings, vaccination checks and daily activity updates.
  2. Audience: Independent Irish dog daycares with approximately 20 to 60 daily places.
  3. Primary CTA: Join the waiting list.
  4. Success measure: Interest expressed through a future measured waiting-list test.
  5. Out of scope: Payments, secure owner accounts, live synchronisation, real messaging and commercial deployment.

The current waiting-list form does not collect actual registrations. It validates the fields in the browser, then shows a message explaining that nothing was sent or saved.

  1. Stage 1 · Task 2

    Initial build

    My prompt asked for

    • Three views named Bookings, Today and Pupdate.
    • A 40-place daily limit counted from real booking records, with the nearest free date suggested when a day is full.
    • Vaccinations checked against the attendance date: amber within 14 days, red when expired, blocking booking and check-in.
    • One-tap Ate, Walked, Played and Napped buttons with timestamps and no duplicate taps.
    • An owner view that shows only that owner's dog.

    What was built

    Testing and result

    • 48 automated checks passed.

  2. Stage 2 · Task 2

    My first review

    My review notes

    • The 60-day alternative-booking search did not match the four-week calendar. Suggested dates must always be reachable and selectable.
    • Suggested dates should explain why they suit, and the booking must use the date that is visibly selected.
    • If localStorage fails, the app must not imply a booking or activity has been saved.
    • The dog selector should be quicker to use one-handed on a phone, searchable and accessible.
    • The owner Pupdate preview should feel like a separate owner screen and say it is not secure sign-in.
    • Staff should be able to see at once which dogs need vaccination action.
    • Layouts must work at 320, 375, 390 and 430px with no sideways scrolling, clipped text or menu overlap.
    • Outdated sample scenarios need a safe way to be refreshed.
    • Regression tests for capacity, duplicates, expiry boundaries, check-in blocking, activities, Pupdate filtering, cancellation, undo and persistence.

    What changed

    • Suggestions limited to the four-week calendar, with up to two options and the reasons each one suits.
    • The Book button names its date and refuses if it no longer matches the selected day.
    • Every save is checked, and messages say “Saved.” or “Not saved” honestly.
    • A searchable dog picker with large one-tap rows.
    • A full-screen owner view labelled “Demo preview, not secure sign-in”.
    • A Vaccination actions list, expired dogs first.
    • A “Bring scenarios up to today” option that keeps all data and can be undone.

    Testing and result

    • 98 automated checks passed.
    • Problems found and fixed: cards 1px too wide at 320px, a squeezed dog name, arrow buttons shrinking, summary labels covered by a decoration, and today's booking counted twice in the vaccination list.

  3. Stage 3 · Task 2

    My final correction review

My testing notes

During development, the Waggy Tails application went through several rounds of technical review. The focus was on whether the three main features worked together and whether staff could complete everyday tasks without unnecessary steps.

The initial review covered the booking calendar, daily capacity limits, vaccination warnings, activity recording and the owner-facing Pupdate view. The core features were present, but two areas needed further attention.

The first was the alternative booking-date feature. The system could search further ahead than the calendar allowed the user to navigate. This was confusing because a suggested date should be something staff can immediately select. The follow-up development prompt therefore focused on making the suggested date and calendar work together.

The second issue involved saving information. The review identified a risk that the application could display a success message even if browser storage failed. This needed to be corrected because staff should be able to trust that a booking or activity has actually been saved.

Further reviews considered vaccination warnings, mobile dog selection, owner-preview clarity and the full journey from booking through to recording an activity and viewing Pupdate.

The final source review indicated that the main calendar and storage concerns had been addressed. The provisional assessment was approximately 46 to 48 out of 50, although this was not an official grade and did not replace physical-phone testing.

What I learned: A feature can be technically present without being easy to use. The alternative-date issue was a good example. The system was trying to help staff find another booking date, but the navigation also needed to support that recommendation. The reviews helped focus the improvements on completing the actual staff workflow rather than simply adding more features.

Testing method: These findings came from source-code review and development iterations. Physical-device testing remains a separate verification step.

Example: What I tried Illustrative

“I started with the booking calendar and selected a day that was already full. I wanted to see whether the system would prevent another booking and offer another date. I then moved to Today, checked the vaccination warnings and tried recording an activity for an eligible dog. Finally, I opened Pupdate to see whether the activity appeared in the dog's daily notes.”

This shows how a test would be recorded. It is not a claim that a physical test was completed.

Issue 1: Alternative booking dates Verified

When I reviewed the first version, I found a mismatch between the alternative-date search and the calendar navigation. The search could consider dates up to 60 days ahead, while the calendar navigation was restricted to four weeks. This could result in a suggested date that the user could not easily reach.

Why this matters. Staff need to act on a suggested date immediately, particularly when taking bookings while dealing with dogs and owners.

Improvement I requested: suggested dates must be reachable, calendar navigation must stay consistent, the displayed date and booking date must match, and the app must explain why a date was offered.

Implemented correction (from the current source).

  • The booking window is defined once as four weeks (today plus 27 days). Alternative dates are only searched inside that window, and a booking beyond it is refused with a reason.
  • Up to two alternatives are offered, the nearest earlier and nearest later date. Each lists the places left, confirms the dog is not already booked and confirms the vaccinations are valid on that day.
  • Selecting an alternative moves the calendar to the right week, highlights that day and shows a notice that the date has changed.
  • The Book button names the full date it will book. If that no longer matches the selected day, the booking is refused.

Issue 2: Save confirmation Verified

In the same review, I identified a risk that the application could show a successful booking or activity update even if localStorage failed. The storage function could catch an error while the calling function still continued to show success.

Why this matters. Staff must be able to trust that a booking or daily activity has actually been recorded.

Improvement I requested: storage operations report success or failure, success messages appear only when the save succeeds, failure gets clear feedback, and nothing gives a misleading confirmation.

Implemented correction (from the current source).

  • The save function writes the data, reads it back, and reports success only if the stored copy matches.
  • Every change (booking, cancellation, check-in, activity, undo, reset and sample-date refresh) returns whether it was saved.
  • Confirmations end with “Saved.” only when the save worked. Otherwise they say “Not saved: this browser is blocking storage, so this will be lost on refresh.”, and a warning banner explains the risk.

Example: What confused me Illustrative

“The suggested alternative date was not always easy to relate to the visible calendar. If I was using the app quickly, I would expect the calendar to move directly to that date instead of making me search for it.”

An illustrative observation based on the earlier confirmed code issue, not a recorded user comment.

Example: What changed Verified

My improvement prompts asked for booking-date consistency and honest storage confirmation. I checked both corrections in the current source; they are described under Issue 1 and Issue 2 above. The calendar now moves directly to a selected suggestion, which addresses the confusion described in the example.

Strengths

  • Bookings: weekly capacity management, booking restrictions and alternative-date handling.
  • Today: vaccination warnings, check-in eligibility and one-tap activity recording.
  • Pupdate: an owner-facing preview displaying daily activity notes for the relevant fictional dog.
  • Fictional sample data, browser persistence, capacity rules and vaccination eligibility.
  • Consistent design, mobile-first layouts and iterative technical review.

Areas for improvement

  • Calendar navigation and suggested alternative dates.
  • Accurate feedback when browser storage fails.
  • Sample-data date handling.
  • Full workflow verification on a physical phone.
  • Clear disclosure that the owner preview is not secure authentication.

The first, second, third and fifth points were addressed in stages 2 and 3 (see sections K and L). Physical-phone verification is still outstanding.

Lessons for future development

  • Build the required workflows before adding visual polish.
  • Use fictional records that make the business rules easy to demonstrate.
  • Test the complete journey from booking to owner update.
  • Check that save confirmations reflect actual saved state.
  • Record at least one genuine usability problem and the change made.
  • Avoid describing a browser prototype as a production-ready platform.

N

Prototype limitations

  • Fictional sample data.
  • Browser-local persistence.
  • No secure owner authentication.
  • No live cross-device synchronisation.
  • No actual owner notifications.
  • No payment processing.
  • No commercial deployment.
  • No real waiting-list collection.

A production product would need a secure backend, authenticated accounts, access controls, data protection work, backups and much broader testing with real daycare teams.
